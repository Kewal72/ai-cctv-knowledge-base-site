    How AI detects people and vehicles¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    The AI draws a box around what it recognises, gives a confidence score, and sends an alert if the score is above the threshold you set.

    At a glance¶

    flowchart LR
    A[Video frame] --> B["AI draws a box<br/>around the object"]
    B --> C["Confidence score<br/>e.g. Person 92%"]
    C --> D{"Above the<br/>threshold?"}
    D -- Yes --> E[Send alert]
    D -- No --> F[Ignore]

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    The right settings decide whether the customer gets trustworthy alerts, too many false alarms, or missed intruders.

    How it works (simple)¶

    • Frames: video is many photos per second, like a flipbook. The AI checks each one.
    • Detection: the AI draws a box around each person or vehicle it recognises.
    • Confidence score: each box shows how sure the AI is, e.g. "Person: 92%."
    • Threshold (sensitivity): the minimum score needed to send an alert.

    Setting the sensitivity¶

    Setting What happens Risk
    Too high sensitivity (low threshold) Catches everything False alarms: dogs or bags reported as people
    Too low sensitivity (high threshold) Very few false alarms Missed events: a real intruder goes unreported
    Balanced Few false alarms, few misses Needs testing at each site

    The two kinds of mistakes¶

    • False alarm: the AI alerts on something that isn't a threat. This is annoying, and the customer stops trusting alerts.
    • Missed event: the AI misses a real person. This is dangerous, because it's what the customer paid to prevent.

    Match the setting to what the customer cares about most. A warehouse at night usually prefers a few extra alerts over missing a thief.

    When the AI struggles¶

    • The person is too far away and looks like a few dots
    • Poor light, or headlights shining into the lens
    • The person is half hidden behind a car or wall
    • The camera is too high, looking straight down, so a person looks like just a head

    Setup tips¶

    • Tune the sensitivity and detection zone at every installation. AI cameras aren't "install and forget."
    • Draw detection zones to cover the area that matters (the wall, the gate) and leave out trees and roads.
    • Mount cameras at about 3–4 m and angle them to see people from the front or side, not from straight above.

    Common mistakes¶

    Problem Wrong fix Right fix
    False alerts from a dog or trees Replace with a costlier camera, or turn AI off Lower sensitivity a little and redraw the detection zone
    Camera misses people at a gate Blame the AI chip or the internet Lower the camera and change the angle
    Customer asks "will it catch everyone?" Promise 100% Explain it depends on distance, light and angle

    Example¶

    A school's boundary-wall cameras sent 30+ alerts a night because of trees. The installer reduced sensitivity a little and redrew the zone to cover only the wall. Alerts dropped to 1–2 a night, all real.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "The camera checks every frame and only alerts when it's confident it sees a person or vehicle. During installation we tune how sensitive it is for your site, so you get real alerts without missing anything important."
