    Adding AI to existing cameras¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    Most customers don't need new cameras to get AI. Check whether the cameras are analog or IP and whether the image quality is good, then upgrade the "brain": an AI DVR/XVR, an AI NVR, or an AI box. Replace only the key or poor cameras.

    Pitch: "Keep your cameras. Upgrade the brain."

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    They get AI alerts at a fraction of the cost of a new system, without throwing away cameras that work.

    Step 1: Analog or IP?¶

    Analog (HD) cameras IP cameras
    Cable Coaxial (round cable with a metal pin) + separate power Network cable (LAN, often PoE)
    Recorder DVR NVR
    Common at Older and budget installations Newer and mid-to-high-end installations
    How to tell Recorder says DVR, XVR, "Turbo HD", "HDCVI" or "AHD" Recorder says NVR; cameras use LAN cables

    Step 2: Choose the upgrade route¶

    Route What we do Good Watch out
    A. AI DVR / XVR (analog) Replace only the old DVR with an AI DVR/XVR Cheapest, no new cabling Usually basic AI, often on some channels only
    B. AI NVR (IP) Replace only the NVR with an AI NVR Keeps all IP cameras With mixed brands (ONVIF), AI may be limited. Test before promising
    C. AI box Add a separate AI device that reads existing video Nothing replaced; good for mixed setups Extra device; needs IP video streams
    D. Replace key cameras AI cameras (ideally with siren and light) at 2–4 key spots Best value; combines with A or B Only covers the key spots

    ONVIF is a common "language" that lets cameras and recorders from different brands work together. Recording usually works across brands, but AI features may not.

    Which route to choose¶

    flowchart TD
    Q1{"Cameras working and<br/>image quality OK?"}
    Q1 -- No --> R0["Replace bad cameras<br/>+ AI cameras at key spots"]
    Q1 -- Yes --> Q2{Analog or IP?}
    Q2 -- Analog --> A["Route A: AI DVR/XVR<br/>+ Route D for key spots"]
    Q2 -- IP --> Q3{"Same brand as the<br/>new AI NVR?"}
    Q3 -- Yes --> B[Route B: AI NVR]
    Q3 -- "No / mixed" --> C["Route C: AI box,<br/>or test ONVIF first"]

    Step 3: Check image quality first¶

    AI is only as good as what the camera sees. If a camera's image is poor, replace that camera.

    Problem Result
    Very old, low-resolution camera People look blurry, AI misses them
    Weak or dead night vision (IR) Dark images at night
    Dirty, scratched or foggy lens Blur and false alarms
    Bad angle (too high, facing the sun) AI can't recognise people

    Site survey checklist¶

    • [ ] Analog (DVR) or IP (NVR)?
    • [ ] Recorder brand, model and age
    • [ ] Number of cameras, brand and resolution
    • [ ] Image quality in the day and at night, for each camera
    • [ ] Cameras in the right places for AI (gate, wall, entrance)?
    • [ ] Hard disk health and recording days
    • [ ] What does the customer actually want? (Night intrusion, gate log, footfall)
    • [ ] Internet available? (Only needed for phone alerts or cloud)

    Common mistakes¶

    • Promising full AI on a mixed-brand system without testing.
    • Ignoring poor night vision on old cameras, then blaming the AI.
    • Quoting a full replacement when an upgrade would do. The customer says no.

    Example¶

    A factory had 16 analog cameras and an 8-year-old DVR, and wanted night intrusion alerts. The survey found 14 cameras with good images and 2 boundary cameras with dead night vision.

    Option What Result
    Replace everything 16 new AI cameras + new NVR + cabling Too expensive; the owner said no
    AI XVR only New AI XVR, keep all 16 cameras Doesn't work: the 2 boundary cameras show black images at night, so the AI can't detect anyone there
    Smart upgrade New AI XVR + replace the 2 boundary cameras with cameras that have working night vision + keep 14 Night alerts work at a fraction of the cost; AMC signed
    Optional add-on Make those 2 replacement cameras ones with siren and light Also scares intruders away, for a small extra cost

    Why not just the AI XVR? AI can't detect what the camera can't see. Upgrade the recorder first, replace only the cameras that can't see properly, and offer deterrence (siren and light) as an optional upsell, not a must.

    Business opportunity¶

    1. Take the list of past customers.
    2. Offer a free "AI upgrade check" (site survey).
    3. Use the checklist and quote an upgrade route.
    4. Add an AMC to every upgrade.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "You don't need to throw away your cameras. We'll check them, replace only the recorder or a couple of key cameras, and you'll get smart alerts on your phone for people and vehicles at a much lower cost."
