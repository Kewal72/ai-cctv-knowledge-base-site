    AI upgrade playbook (Step 1)¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-22

    In one line¶

    For a customer who already has cameras: upgrade the recorder to an AI XVR or AI NVR so every good camera gets AI, and add AI cameras with siren and light only at the few spots that need them.

    Pitch: "Keep your cameras. Upgrade the brain. Add a guard at the weak spots."

    What Step 1 includes¶

    Step 1 is what we sell first: simple, reliable, and easy to demo.

    Feature What it does
    Person/vehicle detection Alerts only for people and vehicles, not trees, dogs or rain
    Intrusion Alert when someone enters a zone (yard, rooftop, store room)
    Line-crossing Alert when someone crosses a line (top of a wall, a gate after closing)
    Loitering Alert when someone stays too long (shopfront at night, back lane)
    Siren + light Scares the intruder away while it's happening

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    They get alerts they can trust, at a fraction of the cost of a new system, without throwing away cameras that work.

    The golden rule: recorder first, AI cameras only where needed¶

    Upgrade the recorder (AI XVR / AI NVR) Add or replace with an AI camera
    What it does Adds AI to the cameras the customer already has Gives AI + siren and light at one spot
    Cost Lowest: one box for many cameras Higher per spot
    Use it for Every site with working cameras Only the weak or high-risk spots (see below)
    Like… One smart supervisor watching all the screens A trained guard with a whistle and torch at the back gate

    Step A: Choose the recorder¶

    flowchart TD
    Q1{"Existing cameras:<br/>analog or IP?"}
    Q1 -- Analog --> X["Replace DVR with<br/>AI XVR"]
    Q1 -- IP --> Q2{"Cameras the same brand<br/>as the new AI NVR?"}
    Q2 -- Yes --> N["Replace NVR with<br/>AI NVR"]
    Q2 -- "No / mixed" --> T["Test ONVIF first.<br/>If AI doesn't work: AI box<br/>or AI cameras at key spots"]

    How to tell analog from IP: analog uses round coaxial cable and a DVR/XVR; IP uses LAN cable and an NVR. See Adding AI to existing cameras.

    Check the AI channel count. A recorder spec like "AI: 4-ch" means only 4 cameras get AI. Count how many cameras need AI and buy a recorder that covers them.

    Step B: Decide camera by camera¶

    Go to each camera and ask these questions in order:

    flowchart TD
    Q1{"Clear image day AND night?<br/>Person big enough?"}
    Q1 -- No --> R["Replace with<br/>AI camera"]
    Q1 -- Yes --> Q2{"High-risk spot where<br/>a siren would help?"}
    Q2 -- Yes --> D["Replace with AI camera<br/>with siren + light"]
    Q2 -- No --> K["Keep it. The AI<br/>recorder adds AI"]

    Where to put an AI camera (with siren and light)¶

    Spot Why
    Boundary walls and back walls Thieves climb here. Line-crossing + siren stops them before they're inside
    Main gate after closing First point of entry
    Godown / warehouse shutters and doors Where goods are stolen from
    Back lanes and side passages Dark, hidden, nobody watching
    Rooftops and terraces A common way in that owners forget
    Shopfront at night Loitering + siren before the shutter is broken

    When an existing camera must be replaced¶

    Problem found in the survey Why the AI recorder can't fix it
    Night vision (IR) dead or weak AI can't detect what the camera can't see
    Very old, low-resolution, blurry People look like blobs; AI misses them
    Too far: person is tiny on screen Below the DORI "detect" level
    One wide camera covering a long wall Far end is too small and outside IR range. Use 2 cameras
    Mixed brand and ONVIF test failed Recording works but AI doesn't
    Mounted too high, looking straight down AI sees only heads. Lower to about 2.5–4 m

    Keep the existing camera when¶

    • The image is clear, day and night.
    • A person at the far end of the area still looks clearly like a person.
    • It's an indoor or low-risk spot (billing counter, office, corridor) where a siren isn't needed.

    Step C: Pick the package¶

    Package What's included Good for
    Basic AI XVR/NVR only, keep all cameras Tight budget, all cameras in good condition
    Standard (recommended) AI XVR/NVR + replace poor cameras + 2 AI cameras with siren and light at the riskiest spots Most warehouses, godowns, shops, factories
    Premium Standard + 4 siren cameras + more coverage Large sites, high-value goods

    Add AMC to every package. See Pricing, packages and AMC.

    The full process¶

    flowchart LR
    A[1. Find lead] --> B[2. Site survey]
    B --> C[3. Decide + quote]
    C --> D[4. Order]
    D --> E[5. Install + set up AI]
    E --> F[6. Test + 2-week tuning]
    F --> G[7. Handover + AMC]

    1. Find leads¶

    • Start with past customers: they already trust us.
    • Offer a free "AI upgrade check" (site survey).
    • Then warehouses, godowns, factories and shops in our area.

    2. Site survey¶

    Visit once in the day and look at the recorder's night footage. Fill in one row per camera:

    Cam Location Analog / IP Brand Day image Night image Risk spot? Decision
    1 Main gate Analog Good Good Yes AI camera + siren
    2 Back wall Analog Good IR dead Yes Replace: AI camera + siren
    3 Billing counter Analog Good Good No Keep

    Also note:

    • [ ] Recorder type, brand, age; number of channels
    • [ ] Hard disk health and recording days
    • [ ] Internet at the site (needed for phone alerts)
    • [ ] UPS for recorder, router and switch
    • [ ] What the customer actually wants: night theft, wall climbing, shopfront

    3. Decide and quote¶

    • Use Step A and Step B above to fill the "Decision" column.
    • Make 3 packages; mark Standard "Recommended".
    • Show the payback: a guard's salary or one theft vs the system price.

    4. Order¶

    • Buy STQC-certified models only (India's CCTV security certification) (to verify with distributor).
    • Recorder and AI cameras from the same brand, so all AI features work.
    • Check the spec sheet: "human/vehicle classification" or "deep learning", and the AI channel count. See Reading a spec sheet.

    5. Install and set up AI¶

    Setting What to do
    Detection type Human/vehicle detection ON; plain motion detection OFF for alerts
    Intrusion zones Cover only the property; leave out trees, roads and neighbours
    Line-crossing Draw where people appear full body; set the direction (outside → inside)
    Loitering Set a time, e.g. 30–60 seconds outside the shop at night (adjust per site)
    Schedule Alerts only in closed hours (e.g. 10 pm – 7 am, as the customer wants)
    Sensitivity Start in the middle; tune during the 2 weeks
    Siren + light Only in closed hours, only on the riskiest cameras
    Recorder Correct date and time; latest firmware; hard disk recording
    Phone app Install on the owner's phone; alerts on; battery saver off for the app
    Backup Save a copy of the settings

    Follow the placement checklist for any new or moved camera.

    6. Test and 2-week tuning¶

    On installation day, test every AI camera and log the result:

    • [ ] Walk across each line: normal, slow, fast, crouching
    • [ ] Walk into each zone from the far end
    • [ ] Stand outside the shop longer than the loitering time
    • [ ] Siren and light go off in closed hours
    • [ ] Alert arrives on the owner's phone within seconds
    • [ ] Repeat the key tests after dark

    For the next 2 weeks, check the alert log every few days:

    What you see Fix
    Too many false alarms Lower sensitivity a little; redraw the zone
    A real person missed Raise sensitivity a little; check distance and IR range
    Alerts in daytime Fix the schedule
    Owner says no alerts Check phone battery saver and app notifications first

    See Why AI fails, and how to fix it.

    7. Handover and AMC¶

    • Show the owner: live view, a test alert, how to switch the siren on or off, how to play back an alert.
    • Tell them one honest limit, e.g. "At night it works well up to about X m."
    • Sign the AMC: regular lens cleaning, zone checks, firmware updates, phone app checks.

    Example¶

    A godown has 8 old analog cameras and a 6-year-old DVR. The owner wants to stop night theft from the back wall.

    Camera Survey finding Decision
    1–5 (inside, office, counter) Good day and night Keep
    6 (main gate) Good, but high-risk AI camera with siren + light
    7 (back wall, 50 m) One wide camera; far end too small, outside IR range Replace with 2 AI cameras with siren + light, one at each end
    8 (side lane) IR dead Replace with AI camera
    DVR Old, no AI Replace with AI XVR

    Result (Standard package): 1 AI XVR, 4 new AI cameras, 5 old cameras kept, AMC included. Night alerts work along the whole back wall, and the siren scares intruders before they reach the goods.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Replacing every camera when a recorder upgrade would do. The customer says no to the price.
    • Only upgrading the recorder when some cameras can't see at night. The AI then "fails" and we get blamed.
    • Buying a recorder whose AI channels don't cover the cameras that need AI.
    • Mixing brands and promising full AI without testing ONVIF first.
    • Siren on all day, or on every camera. The owner gets annoyed and switches it off.
    • Skipping the night test.
    • Not turning off battery saver on the owner's phone.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "You don't need to throw away your cameras. We'll check each one, upgrade the recorder so they all get smart alerts, and put a camera with a siren and light at your weak spots, like the back wall. You'll get alerts only for real people, and thieves get scared off before they take anything."
