    Camera placement for AI¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-11

    In one line¶

    AI needs to see the target big enough, from the right angle, in good light. Most "AI doesn't work" complaints are really placement problems.

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    Correct placement is the difference between reliable alerts and missed intruders or constant false alarms, even with the best camera.

    1. Height and angle¶

       TOO HIGH (8m+)             RIGHT (about 2.5–4m)          TOO LOW (under 2m)
        📷                           📷
        │                             ╲
        │ looks straight              ╲  sees people          📷──── easy to hit,
        │ down → only                  ╲ from the side           block or turn
        ▼ heads visible          🧍     ╲→ full body
       🧍                                                     🧍
    Placement Problem
    Too high Sees only heads and shoulders; AI can't recognise a person
    Too low Easy to vandalise, cover or turn; passing people block the view
    Right About 2.5–4 m (check the install guide), tilted down a little so people appear full body, from the side or front
    • Keep sky out of the picture, or only a small strip.
    • Point the camera along the area people walk through, not straight across it at close range.

    2. Distance and size on screen (DORI)¶

    The further away a person is, the smaller they look. Pixels per metre measures how much detail the camera captures. The DORI standard:

    Level Pixels per metre What you can do Needed for
    Detect 25 See that someone is there Intrusion, line-crossing
    Observe 62 See clothing, actions Smart search by colour
    Recognise 125 Recognise a known person Checking footage of staff
    Identify 250 Identify a stranger beyond doubt Face recognition, ANPR

    Spec sheets list DORI distances for each camera. They're "up to" figures, so plan for less.

    Lens choice¶

    Lens Sees Best for
    Wide (e.g. 2.8 mm) Wide area, short distance Rooms, shopfronts, small yards
    Narrow (e.g. 6 mm, 8 mm) Narrow area, further Long walls, driveways, far gates
    Varifocal / motorised zoom Adjustable at installation Fine-tuning AI coverage

    3. Light¶

    Problem Fix
    Facing the sun or bright gates Change the angle; use WDR cameras
    Headlights shining into the lens Angle away from the road; use ANPR cameras at gates
    Area beyond the IR range Longer IR range, add a light, or colour night vision
    IR reflection (camera too close to a wall, roof edge or pole) Mount away from surfaces; clean spider webs regularly

    4. Drawing zones and lines¶

    • Draw lines where the person appears full body, not at the edge of the picture.
    • Include the approach area in intrusion zones, not just the wall.
    • Leave out trees, roads and areas outside the property.
    • Overlap coverage at corners.

    5. Obstacles¶

    • Trees and plants grow and can block the view in months.
    • Parked vehicles, stacked goods, poles and signboards.
    • Moving things in the zone: flags, banners, fans.

    Placement by feature¶

    Feature Height Distance Key tip
    Intrusion / line-crossing About 2.5–4 m Within detect range Full body visible; keep sky out
    Face recognition About head height (slightly above) Close: identify range Narrow entry point; avoid backlight
    ANPR Low to medium, facing the plate Close: identify range Slow vehicles, small angle, 1 lane
    People counting Ceiling, looking down Above the entrance Dedicated overhead camera
    Smart search About 2.5–4 m Within observe range Clear view of clothing colours

    Common mistakes¶

    • One wide camera trying to cover a long wall.
    • Mounting too high "to see everything."
    • Ignoring the IR range at the far end of the scene.
    • Camera mounted right under a roof edge, causing IR reflection.

    Example¶

    A warehouse used one wide (2.8 mm) camera at a corner to cover a 60 m wall. At the far end, people were too small to detect, and it was beyond the IR range at night. The fix: 2 cameras, one at each end, each covering half the wall with a narrower lens and enough IR range. The whole wall was then covered day and night.

    Placement checklist¶

    • [ ] Height about 2.5–4 m (or as per feature)
    • [ ] People visible full body, from the side or front
    • [ ] Target within the camera's DORI range for the feature
    • [ ] Right lens for the distance
    • [ ] Not facing the sun or headlights; little or no sky
    • [ ] Within IR range at night; away from walls, roofs and webs
    • [ ] Zones and lines drawn properly; trees and roads left out
    • [ ] No obstacles; corners overlap

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "Where we put the camera matters as much as which camera we use. We'll place each one at the right height and distance so the AI clearly sees people coming, day and night. That's why we do a site visit before quoting."
