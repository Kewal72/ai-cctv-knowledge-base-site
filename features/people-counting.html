    People counting¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    The camera counts people going in and out through a line at the entrance, turning CCTV into business reports: footfall, peak hours, occupancy and conversion rate.

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    It helps owners run the business better, not just protect it. This is how to sell AI CCTV to customers who say "I don't have a theft problem."

    How it works (simple)¶

    flowchart LR
    A[Virtual line at the entrance] --> B[Count IN]
    A --> C[Count OUT]
    B --> D["People inside now<br/>= IN − OUT"]
    C --> D
    B --> E["Reports: footfall,<br/>peak hours, conversion"]

    What the owner gets¶

    Report What it tells them Who cares
    Footfall Visitors per day, week, month Shops, malls, showrooms
    Peak hours Busiest times of day Staff planning
    Occupancy People inside right now Malls, temples, event halls, offices
    Heatmap Where people go or stand most, shown as colours Retail product placement
    Queue alert Too many people waiting at billing Supermarkets, banks, hospitals

    Conversion rate¶

    Number
    Visitors (from camera) 500
    Bills (from billing software) 100
    Conversion rate 100 ÷ 500 = 20%

    If visitors go up but bills don't, there's a problem with staff, pricing or stock.

    Types of counting cameras¶

    Overhead counting camera Normal AI camera with a counting line
    Mounted Ceiling, looking straight down at the entrance Wall, at an angle
    Accuracy High, even in crowds Good when not crowded, drops with groups
    Cost Higher (dedicated device) Lower (uses existing AI features)
    Best for Malls, busy stores, accurate reports Small shops, rough footfall numbers

    For detecting intruders, a straight-down view is bad. For counting at a door, a dedicated overhead camera is best, because it's designed for that view.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Groups walking close together get counted as fewer people.
    • Trolleys, prams and kids confuse the count.
    • Very wide entrances may need 2 cameras.
    • Staff going in and out get counted too. Check whether the system can filter them out.

    Example¶

    A clothing store saw 600 visitors on Sunday but only 60 bills (10%), compared to 25% on weekdays. The owner added 2 staff on Sundays and conversion rose to 18%. The camera paid for itself in sales.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "This camera counts every customer who walks in. You'll know your busiest hours, how many visitors actually buy, and whether a new offer is bringing more people in, all on your phone."
