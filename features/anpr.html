    Number-plate reading (ANPR)¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    ANPR (Automatic Number Plate Recognition, also called LPR) reads vehicle number plates automatically to log vehicles, open gates for known vehicles, or alert on banned ones.

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    It replaces the paper register at the gate, speeds up entry for residents and employees, and gives a searchable record of every vehicle with photo, date and time.

    How it works (simple)¶

    1. Spot the vehicle: the AI detects a car, bike or truck.
    2. Find the plate on the vehicle.
    3. Read the characters (OCR), e.g. MH 12 AB 1234.
    4. Check the list and act: log it, alert the guard, or open the boom barrier.
    flowchart LR
    A[Vehicle arrives] --> B[Find the plate]
    B --> C["Read characters<br/>MH 12 AB 1234"]
    C --> D{Check lists}
    D -- Allow list --> E[Open barrier]
    D -- Block list --> F[Alert guard]
    D -- Not on any list --> G["Log it, guard<br/>approves entry"]

    Uses¶

    Use What happens
    Allow list A resident's or employee's vehicle arrives and the barrier opens automatically
    Block list A banned or suspicious vehicle arrives and the guard gets an alert
    Entry and exit log Number, photo, date and time saved for every vehicle
    Truck tracking Know when each truck came in, went out and how long it stayed
    Parking Track parking time for billing or overstay alerts

    Best customers: housing societies, factories, warehouses, logistics parks, hospitals, malls, schools, office campuses.

    India-specific challenges¶

    Problem Example
    Fancy or non-standard fonts Designer plates, stickers
    Dirty, faded or broken plates Mud, dust, bent plates
    Two-line plates Bikes, autos, trucks
    Hand-painted truck plates Painted on the body, often worn
    Different plate colours White (private), yellow (commercial), green (EV), black (rental)

    Newer HSRP plates (High Security Registration Plates) use a standard font and are much easier to read.

    Setup tips¶

    1. Use a dedicated ANPR camera. A normal wide camera covering the whole gate can't read plates reliably.
    2. Install at a slow point: a gate, barrier or speed breaker, not an open road.
    3. Keep the angle small: face the plate as straight as possible.
    4. One lane per camera (in-lane and out-lane = 2 cameras).
    5. Follow the camera's recommended distance, so the plate is big enough on screen.
    6. For night, use cameras with IR light and fast shutter speed to avoid headlight glare and motion blur.

    How accurate is it?¶

    • Controlled gate (slow vehicles, good angle, clean HSRP plates): very accurate.
    • Open roads, speeding vehicles, dirty or fancy plates, bad angles: accuracy drops a lot.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Promising plate reading from an existing wide gate camera.
    • Installing where vehicles pass at speed.
    • One camera trying to cover two lanes.
    • Promising 100% accuracy. Muddy or fancy plates may need a manual check.

    Example¶

    A 400-flat housing society replaced its paper register with 2 ANPR cameras (in-lane and out-lane) at the boom barrier, with a speed breaker before it. Residents' vehicles open the barrier automatically, visitors are captured with a photo and approved by the guard, and banned vehicles trigger an alert. The committee can search records in seconds.

    Upsell: ANPR + boom barrier + monthly AMC.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "The camera reads every vehicle number automatically. Residents' cars open the gate by themselves, visitors get logged with a photo and time, and you can search any vehicle in seconds. No more paper register. It works best at the gate where vehicles slow down."
