    Intrusion, line-crossing and loitering¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    You draw a zone or a line on the camera's screen, and the AI alerts when a person or vehicle enters, crosses or stays too long, while ignoring animals, trees and rain.

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    These are the features customers buy most. They warn the owner about intruders while it's happening, and with a siren or light they can scare intruders away before anything is stolen.

    How it works (simple)¶

    Feature What you draw Alerts when… Like…
    Intrusion A zone (box) Someone enters it An invisible "no entry" carpet
    Line-crossing A line Someone crosses it, in the chosen direction (A→B, B→A or both) An invisible laser beam
    Loitering A zone + a time limit Someone stays longer than the limit A shopkeeper who notices someone standing outside too long

    When to use each¶

    Feature Good for
    Intrusion Warehouse yards, godowns, rooftops, server or store rooms
    Line-crossing Tops of boundary walls, gates after closing, one-way entries and exits
    Loitering Shopfronts after closing, ATMs, cash counters, parking, back lanes, outside school gates

    Loitering is useful because thieves often check out a place first. It can warn the owner before anything happens.

    Setup tips¶

    1. Schedule: turn alerts on only when the place is closed (e.g. 10pm–7am). Otherwise staff trigger alerts all day.
    2. Target type: choose person, vehicle or both. Wall = person only. Gate after hours = both.
    3. Intrusion delay: where available, require someone to be inside the zone for a few seconds (e.g. 2–3 s) before alerting.
    4. Line direction: set the direction to match the threat, e.g. outside → inside for a wall.
    5. Actions on alert: phone notification, siren or voice warning, flashing light, recording, NVR alarm.

    Active deterrence cameras¶

    Cameras with a built-in speaker and flashing light can warn intruders automatically ("You are in a restricted area"). They don't just record the thief, they scare him away. This is a strong selling point.

    Common mistakes¶

    • No schedule: alerts fire all day from staff, and the customer turns them off.
    • Wrong target type: vehicle alerts on a wall where only people matter, causing extra alerts.
    • Line drawn in both directions when only one direction matters, which doubles the alerts.
    • Zones that include trees or a public road, which cause unnecessary alerts.

    Example¶

    A textile godown had small thefts at night through the back wall. The setup:

    • Line-crossing on the back wall (outside → inside, person only)
    • Intrusion on the loading yard (10pm–7am)
    • Loitering in the back lane (alert after 2 minutes)
    • Cameras with siren and flashing light

    When someone climbed the wall, the camera flashed and played a warning, the owner got a phone alert with a clip, and the person ran away before taking anything.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "Normal CCTV shows you the theft the next morning. With AI, we draw an invisible line on your wall. If anyone crosses it at night, the camera sounds a warning, flashes a light and sends a video to your phone, so the thief runs away before taking anything."
