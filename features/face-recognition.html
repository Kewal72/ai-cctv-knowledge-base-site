    Face recognition¶

    Draft

    Last updated: 2026-10-10

    In one line¶

    Face recognition identifies who a person is by comparing their face with a list of enrolled people. It only works with a close, front-facing, well-lit view of the face.

    Why it matters to the customer¶

    It can alert the owner when a VIP, an employee, a known shoplifter or a stranger arrives. It's also the feature most often oversold, so setting the right expectations avoids unhappy customers.

    Face detection vs face recognition¶

    What it means Example
    Face detection "There's a face here" "A person entered"
    Face recognition "This is Ramesh" "Ramesh (employee) entered at 9:02 am"

    How it works (simple)¶

    1. Enrol: upload clear photos of people into the system.
    2. Faceprint: the system turns each face into a unique number code (distance between the eyes, shape of the nose and jaw).
    3. Compare: each new face is compared with the enrolled list.
    4. Match score: e.g. "Ramesh, 94% match." Above the threshold = a match.
    flowchart LR
    A[Enrol clear photos] --> B["Faceprint<br/>number code"]
    C[Person walks past] --> D[Faceprint of the new face]
    B --> E{Compare}
    D --> E
    E -- "Match above threshold" --> F["Name + alert<br/>e.g. VIP arrived"]
    E -- No match --> G[Stranger]

    Types of lists¶

    • Allow list: employees, family, VIP customers
    • Block list: known shoplifters or banned people
    • Stranger alert: anyone not on the allow list

    When to use it / when not to¶

    Good for Not good for
    Office or factory attendance and door access Wide cameras covering a whole gate or compound
    VIP and shoplifter alerts at a showroom entrance Cameras mounted high, looking down
    Stranger alerts at a home door or pedestrian gate Places where people wear helmets or masks
    Narrow entry points: doors, turnstiles, corridors Crowded areas with fast-moving people

    Setup tips¶

    • Use a dedicated face recognition camera. A normal wide camera is not a face recognition camera.
    • Install it at a narrow point (door, turnstile, corridor) at about head height, facing people as they walk in.
    • Avoid backlight, e.g. a bright doorway behind the person.
    • Use clear, recent, front-facing photos for enrolment.

    Why it fails¶

    Problem Why
    Too far away The face is too small to measure
    Side angle or from above It needs the face nearly straight on
    Mask, helmet, cap, sunglasses Key parts of the face are hidden
    Backlight The face appears dark
    Crowds Faces overlap and move fast
    Poor enrolment photos Weak faceprint

    Privacy and legal¶

    • A face is personal data, protected under India's DPDP Act.
    • Put up clear signage that cameras and face recognition are in use.
    • Get consent from employees for attendance or access systems.
    • Keep the data secure, limit access, and don't keep it longer than needed.
    • For public spaces, schools or large-scale use, the customer should take legal advice.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Promising face recognition from an existing gate or wall camera.
    • Installing the face camera too high or at a side angle.
    • Skipping signage and consent.

    Example¶

    • Works: a jewellery showroom puts a face recognition camera above the entrance door at head height, with a block list of known shoplifters and an allow list of VIP customers. The manager gets alerts like "VIP: Mr. Sharma has arrived."
    • Doesn't work: a housing society wants face recognition from a 4th-floor camera covering the whole main gate. Faces are too small, seen from above, and many people wear helmets. Better: number-plate reading for vehicles at the main gate, plus a face recognition camera at the pedestrian gate.

    How to explain it to a customer¶

    "Face recognition can tell you exactly who walked in, like a VIP customer or a known shoplifter. But it needs a special camera at the door, at face height, where people walk straight towards it. A far or high camera can't do it, so we'll place it where it actually works."
